Teams are asking whether recurring uses of generative AI in standard workplace software need formal assurance. Some cases are hard to classify. How should the branch handle these enquiries?

## Brief

**Option brief verification status:** Needs revision 

**Decision and recommendation**

The lead must decide how the branch will triage recurring generative-AI (Gen AI) uses in standard workplace software and when to refer them for formal assurance. Adopt an interim, risk-based triage: record every recurring use in the branch’s AI-tool register, treat it as potentially in scope unless the branch documents that it is unmodified, vendor-intended standard functionality, and refer uncertain or higher-impact cases to the AI Advisory Group (AIAG). This is recommended because the policy both includes Gen AI in standard products and exempts standard AI functionality; the reviewed material does not resolve the boundary for drafting, summarising, transcription, coding or similar uses.

**Problem and policy context**

The *ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, p. 3, **Scope**, expressly includes Gen AI capabilities in standard products, but exempts “standard usage” as intended and provided by the vendor, without modifications or special customisations.[doc:turn1doc2] An “AI initiative” is a project or program using AI for a specific operational outcome (*ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, p. 4, **Key terms**).[doc:turn1doc2] This leaves unresolved whether repeated individual use, basic configuration, shared workflows, or consequential reliance creates an initiative.

If in scope, all initiatives must be assessed at every stage, using the self-assessment tool and AIAG contestability process (*ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, p. 13, **AI Assurance Framework**).[doc:turn1doc2] Directorate oversight is broader: CIOs/Heads of Corporate must oversee AI tools and maintain a register (*ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, pp. 7–8, **Roles and responsibilities / ACT AI Advisory Group**).[doc:turn1doc2] Separately, staff must regularly review AI guidance for Gen AI use (*Acceptable Use of ICT Resources Policy*, p. 8, **2.1.4 Generative Artificial Intelligence, clause 22**) and protect personal information (*Acceptable Use of ICT Resources Policy*, pp. 11–12, **2.3.1–2.3.2**).[doc:turn1doc1]

**Options and trade-offs**

1. **Treat all recurring Gen AI use as in scope.** Maximises assurance and consistency, but imposes substantial effort on teams and may over-apply the framework to clearly standard, low-risk functions.

2. **Treat standard-product use as exempt unless modified.** Minimises administrative effort, but risks misclassification where use serves a specific outcome, involves sensitive data or consequential decisions.

3. **Interim risk-based triage (recommended).** Apply the documented standard-use test; escalate ambiguity, customisation, sensitive information, material impacts or embedded workflow reliance. This balances workload against the risk that the exemption is incorrectly applied; the trade-off is temporary inconsistency until central guidance is confirmed.

**First step and evaluation**

The branch policy owner should create a short triage record and seek AIAG advice on the unresolved boundary, with CIO/Head of Corporate and privacy, security and legal advisers confirming escalation triggers. Success is 100% of recurring Gen AI uses recorded and classified with a documented rationale; review if any use is found to have bypassed required assurance or caused a privacy, security or material-impact incident. 

 ### Verification notes
The brief identifies the decision, presents three realistic options, recommends an interim triage, and proposes a first step. However, revision is required to separate policy requirements from proposed branch practice and analysis, and to make the implementation boundary explicit.

1. **Overstates the register requirement as a recurring-use rule.** Draft wording: “record every recurring use in the branch’s AI-tool register”. Supporting evidence/gap: *ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, pp. 7–8, **Roles and responsibilities / ACT AI Advisory Group**, supports CIOs/Heads of Corporate maintaining “a register of all AI tools used in their directorates,” but does not expressly prescribe a separate entry for every recurring use or define the classification unit for shared tools. Change needed: label this as a proposed interim control, or amend it to “record every relevant AI tool and the branch’s recurring use of it, pending confirmation of the appropriate register unit.”

2. **The recommended triage is not a policy requirement but is written alongside mandatory rules.** Draft wording: “treat it as potentially in scope unless the branch documents that it is unmodified, vendor-intended standard functionality, and refer uncertain or higher-impact cases to the AI Advisory Group (AIAG).” Supporting evidence/gap: the policy’s scope and exemption support the standard-use test (*ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, p. 3, **Scope**), but do not require a presumption of inclusion, branch documentation in this form, or referral of uncertain/higher-impact cases to AIAG. The AIAG remit is to assess “in scope” initiatives (*ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, pp. 7–8, **Roles and responsibilities / ACT AI Advisory Group**). Change needed: expressly mark the whole triage as “proposed interim branch practice,” and state that AIAG referral is an escalation proposed by the branch, not a cited policy trigger.

3. **The assurance pathway is compressed and slightly imprecise.** Draft wording: “using the self-assessment tool and AIAG contestability process”. Supporting evidence: *ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, p. 13, **AI Assurance Framework**, supports assessment at every stage, use of the self-assessment tool, submission to AIAG for contestability review, and approval through regular DDTS or directorate-specific project lifecycle governance, or AIAG risk-management recommendations. Change needed: distinguish these steps accurately and avoid implying that AIAG contestability is the sole approval route.

4. **Higher-impact and sensitive-data escalation is a proposed risk control, not an express exemption rule.** Draft wording: “escalate ambiguity, customisation, sensitive information, material impacts or embedded workflow reliance.” Supporting evidence/gap: the policy requires safeguards including privacy/security, human review and intervention, and contestability where impacts are significant (*ACT Government Artificial Intelligence Policy*, pp. 12–13, **Ethics principles** and **ACT Public Service staff requirements**). The findings do not expressly map those circumstances to whether a standard functionality loses its exemption or must follow the full assurance pathway. Change needed: retain the escalation as a proposed precaution, state that the source material does not resolve whether these factors alter exemption status, and flag the issue for central/manual confirmation.

5. **The brief omits a separate baseline that applies even when the AI Assurance Framework is not triggered.** Draft wording: “Separately, staff must regularly review AI guidance for Gen AI use ... and protect personal information”. Supporting evidence: *Acceptable Use of ICT Resources Policy*, p. 8, **2.1.4 Generative Artificial Intelligence, clause 22**, requires regular review of relevant guidance; pp. 11–12, **2.3.1–2.3.2 Information security / Information privacy, clauses 64–67**, impose information-protection and privacy obligations. The findings also establish application to ordinary workplace systems, cloud resources and government accounts (pp. 5 and 7, **Application / Official use / Access to ICT resources**). Change needed: add an explicit “always applies” baseline covering authorised official use, information handling, privacy/security, and continuing Gen AI guidance review, whether or not the use is in scope for assurance.

6. **The handling route for questionable use or incidents is missing.** Supporting evidence: *Acceptable Use of ICT Resources Policy*, pp. 15–16 and 19–20, **Security incident reporting / Compliance / Responsibilities**, requires prompt reporting of inappropriate or prohibited use, data spills, breaches or leaks, and gives supervisors escalation responsibilities. Change needed: add an operational branch route: suspected misuse, data spill, breach or leak follows the existing incident-reporting and supervisory escalation process, separately from classification as an AI initiative.

7. **The first step is sensible but its authority and unresolved issue need clearer treatment.** Draft wording: “seek AIAG advice on the unresolved boundary, with CIO/Head of Corporate and privacy, security and legal advisers confirming escalation triggers.” Supporting evidence/gap: the research identifies unresolved boundaries for drafting, summarising, transcription, coding, configuration, consequential reliance and shared workflows. It does not establish that these advisers or AIAG are formally required to confirm those triggers. Change needed: label this as the proposed first step and identify the unresolved implementation questions explicitly, especially the boundary between “generative AI capabilities” in standard products and “standard usage of AI functionalities,” and whether repeated individual or shared use is an “AI initiative.”

8. **The evaluation measure is analysis, not a policy requirement.** Draft wording: “Success is 100% of recurring Gen AI uses recorded and classified with a documented rationale”. Supporting evidence/gap: no supplied finding sets a 100% target or requires this exact documentation. Change needed: identify it as a proposed performance measure, not a policy obligation; define who owns the denominator and how shared tools and newly introduced vendor features are counted.

9. **Citation review.** The material policy claims generally include the required document title, page number and section heading, with the supplied source markers [doc:turn1doc1] and [doc:turn1doc2]. The following require manual source checking before approval: whether “all AI tools” in the register is intended to capture every recurring use; whether “AIAG advice” is an available route for boundary questions; whether the proposed escalation factors change exemption status; and whether the described AIAG contestability wording accurately reflects the full approval sequence. No unsupported policy claim should be presented as mandatory after revision. Human review remains required for the proposed interim controls and the unresolved classification boundary.
