# Source review · Brief-only retest

**Case:** Compare three ways to review AI-generated summaries of public reports before using them in briefs. See the [brief-only retest](docs/WORKFLOW-TEST-REPORTS.md#brief-only-retest).

**Proposed disposition:** Revise the policy context before finalising the brief. Keep the three options and the proposed risk-based recommendation. The project owner makes the final decision.

| Claim or issue | Source check | Change for the final brief |
|---|---|---|
| Standard commercial software and policy scope | [AI Policy, Scope and Key terms, pp. 3–4](docs/ACT-Government-Artificial-Intelligence-Policy.pdf#page=3) expressly includes AI initiatives that use generative capabilities supplied in unmodified commercial products. The definition refers to a project or program. | State the express scope criterion. Leave classification of the proposed team activity open. Remove any inference that standard commercial packaging creates an exemption for generative AI. |
| Human review and a second reviewer | [AI Policy, ACT Public Service staff requirements, p. 13](docs/ACT-Government-Artificial-Intelligence-Policy.pdf#page=13) requires human review and intervention for decisions made by AI systems. The provision specifies no independent second reviewer for generated summaries. | Limit the requirement to AI-made decisions. Label author checking and colleague review thresholds as proposed team controls. |
| Formal assurance | [AI Policy, AI Assurance Framework, p. 13](docs/ACT-Government-Artificial-Intelligence-Policy.pdf#page=13) sets out self-assessment, contestability and lifecycle assessment for in-scope initiatives. It calls for regular review of established solutions. | Keep initiative assurance separate from checks on individual outputs. Avoid saying the framework sets no review frequency without specifying that the point concerns individual summaries. |
| ICT guidance and cloud approval | [ICT policy v2.13, s. 2.1.4, p. 8](docs/Acceptable_ICT_Use_Policy.pdf#page=8) directs staff to keep up with relevant AI guidance. [Section 2.3.6, p. 13](docs/Acceptable_ICT_Use_Policy.pdf#page=13) requires approval to engage cloud providers for official purposes or transfer OFFICIAL information to them. | Make tool authorisation and the handling of the report and accompanying prompt preconditions for implementation. Leave tool-specific approval and local requirements for the responsible policy owner to confirm. |
| Accountability and the proposed review threshold | The brief cites several pages for general principles and then infers when author-only checking is defensible. | Present the author check and risk threshold as the team's proposed design. Use a concrete trigger such as whether a material error could change the advice or a decision. |

## Decision to put in the brief

Ask the lead to endorse a pilot in which authors check every material claim against the original report and a colleague checks summaries where an error could materially change advice or a decision. Before the pilot starts, confirm tool authorisation and any applicable local requirements. Give the team a one-page procedure that names who checks the summary and how they record corrections or escalate a doubtful claim.
